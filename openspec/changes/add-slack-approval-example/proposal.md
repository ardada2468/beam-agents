## Why

The approval loop is built end-to-end on the runtime side and then stops at a topic nobody fronts. An activation stages a `kind = APPROVAL` intent via [`request_approval`](../../../src/beam_agents/core/context.py:559) ([`AgentContext` variant](../../../src/beam_agents/core/context.py:211)), the DoFn suspends with a fail-closed HITL deadline, [`WriteIntents`](../../../src/beam_agents/actions/write_intents.py:237) lands the intent on the outbox, and the effector [routes approval-kind intents verbatim to the approval channel](../../../src/beam_agents/effector/service.py:360) — after which nothing happens until the [`HitlPolicy`](../../../src/beam_agents/hitl.py:136) timer denies. The resume half exists too: [`AgentEnvelope.Approval`](../../../protos/beam_agents.proto:132) re-injected on the same key resumes the suspended activation, and the conformance suite proves it with scripted envelopes. What no user can see today is the shape of the thing in the middle: the out-of-pipeline surface that turns an approval intent into a question a human can answer and the answer back into an envelope. Without a worked example, every adopter re-derives the same non-obvious contract — key by `entity_key`, honor `expires_at_ms` at the surface (not just in the pipeline), tolerate the effector's at-least-once notification, serialize deterministically — and gets at least one of those wrong.

This change ships that example: a small Slack approval surface, in the examples surface established by sibling change `add-docs-site` (C24), that consumes approval-kind intents, posts an interactive Block Kit message with approve/deny buttons, publishes the operator's verdict as an `AgentEnvelope` carrying `Approval`, and treats expired intents as never actionable — the same fail-closed reading the runtime takes ([`intent_expired`](../../../src/beam_agents/hitl.py:63)).

## What Changes

- **New example application `examples/slack_approval/`** — a standalone asyncio service, outside the wheel and outside the pipeline, importing no Beam. It reuses the effector's transport seams rather than growing its own: intents arrive through the [`IntentSource`](../../../src/beam_agents/effector/sources.py:44) protocol ([Kafka](../../../src/beam_agents/effector/sources.py:126) / [Pub/Sub](../../../src/beam_agents/effector/sources.py:196) / [in-memory](../../../src/beam_agents/effector/sources.py:59)), and approval envelopes leave through the [`MessageSink`](../../../src/beam_agents/effector/sinks.py:26) protocol keyed by raw `entity_key` — the same keying `WriteIntents` and the effector's result sink use, so the envelope lands on the partition of the suspended key.
- **Consume → post.** For each delivered intent with `kind == APPROVAL` (any other kind is skipped and committed — the surface may be pointed at the effector's `approvals_to` channel or, in a minimal demo, directly at the outbox), the surface posts one Slack message with Block Kit approve/deny buttons whose action value carries `intent_id`, hex `entity_key`, and `expires_at_ms`. The offset is committed only after the post succeeds, so a crash re-delivers rather than loses. Duplicate notifications from the effector's publish-before-complete ordering are collapsed by `intent_id` within the process, best-effort across restarts.
- **Click → envelope.** A button interaction publishes a deterministically serialized `AgentEnvelope` with `payload = approval` (`intent_id`, `approved`, `approver` = the Slack user id, `decided_at_ms`) keyed by `entity_key` to the pipeline's approvals input topic, then edits the Slack message to show the verdict and remove the buttons. The pipeline stays the arbiter: a late or duplicate envelope is orphaned by `_resume`, never double-applied, so the surface needs no distributed dedup of its own.
- **TTL honored at the surface.** An intent already past `expires_at_ms` at consume time is posted as a non-interactive "expired" notice; a pending message whose expiry passes is edited to expired and its buttons removed by a periodic sweep; a click that races expiry is re-checked at decision time and refused — no envelope is published for an expired intent, matching the fail-closed layers on both sides of it ([`refuse_expired`](../../../src/beam_agents/hitl.py:74) at the effector, deadline/expiry admission in `_resume`).
- **Slack transport = Socket Mode**, behind a `SlackGateway` protocol seam with a scripted in-memory fake. Socket Mode needs no public HTTPS endpoint and therefore no request-signature verification code, which keeps the example runnable on a laptop against docker compose. The docs page covers the HTTP-webhook alternative honestly (signing-secret verification, its replay window) without implementing it.
- **Doc-contract test.** `docs/examples/slack-approval.md` (under the C24 docs surface) documents the demo; `tests/examples/` holds it to that contract the way [`test_failure_streak_alarm.py`](../../../tests/examples/test_failure_streak_alarm.py:1) holds `docs/errors.md`: an offline unit leg drives the whole surface loop with FakeLLM-staged intents and the fake gateway (no docker, no slack-sdk), and an `-m integration` leg closes the real loop over compose Redpanda — FakeLLM agent suspends on `request_approval`, the intent crosses real Kafka, the surface posts and receives a scripted approve, and the envelope it published resumes the suspended activation.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

- `slack-approval-example`: the example approval surface — approval-intent consumption and Block Kit posting, verdict-to-envelope publication keyed by `entity_key`, fail-closed TTL handling at the surface, the Socket Mode gateway seam, and the offline + compose-Kafka doc-contract tests.

### Modified Capabilities

None. The example consumes only existing, documented seams — the effector's source/sink protocols, `hitl.intent_expired`, and the proto bindings — and changes no runtime, effector, or wire behavior. If implementation surfaces a needed seam change (none is anticipated), that becomes its own change.
